From City-Based Franchising to Open Circuits

Launched by Blizzard Entertainment in 2018, the Overwatch League (OWL) was built on a bold, traditional sports model. It featured city-based home-and-away franchises (like the New York Excelsior and Seoul Dynasty), multi-million dollar slot buy-ins, and a localized global framework.

Despite early hype and massive investments, the league faced severe headwinds over the years—including pandemic disruptions, sponsorship losses, and shifting publisher strategies. By late 2023, team owners voted to terminate the Overwatch League partnership, bringing an official end to the franchise era.

Rather than dying out, competitive Overwatch successfully pivoted. Blizzard transitioned the scene into the Overwatch Champions Series (OWCS), a more sustainable, open-circuit model that allows independent teams and players around the globe to freely compete in tiered tournaments once again.
